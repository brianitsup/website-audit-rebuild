# Audit Report Template (Deliverable A — for the website owner)

Audience: the business owner or manager. Assume no technical background. Every technical term used gets a one-line plain-language gloss the first time. Every recommendation connects to a business outcome. Keep the main body readable in ~15 minutes; depth lives in the appendix and the findings register.

Structure:

```markdown
# Website Audit Report — <Site name>
Prepared for: <owner/org>  ·  Prepared by: <auditor>  ·  Date: <date>
Website reviewed: <URL>  ·  Review period: <dates>  ·  Scope: publicly accessible pages only

## 1. Executive summary
One page maximum. What state the site is in (one honest sentence), the 3–5
findings that matter most to the business, the headline recommendation
(e.g., "targeted fixes" vs. "rebuild"), and expected business benefit.
Write this LAST.

## 2. About this review
What was examined (N pages, which areas), how (public review, no logins),
and what this review is not (not a penetration test, not a guarantee of
search rankings). Sets honest expectations — protects both parties.

## 3. Your website today — overview
Current sitemap (simple tree), what the site offers visitors, platform it
runs on, and a factual snapshot: pages, forms, features, integrations.

## 4. What's working well
Genuine strengths with evidence. Content worth keeping, features that work,
brand assets to preserve. (If this section is hard to fill, say so honestly
— but look properly first.)

## 5. Key issues and risks
The top findings, grouped by business impact rather than technical category:
  - Issues costing you visitors or enquiries
  - Issues hurting your visibility on Google
  - Issues creating security or legal risk
  - Issues making the site expensive to maintain
Each: what we found (plain language) → evidence (URL / screenshot / observation)
→ why it matters to the business → what to do about it.
Severity labels: Critical / High / Medium / Low, with a one-line legend.

## 6. Recommendations and priorities
The prioritised action list. For each: recommendation, priority, estimated
complexity (Small / Medium / Large — explain these mean effort bands, not
prices), and dependencies. End with the overall recommended path and why.

## 7. Suggested implementation phases
Phase 0: urgent fixes on the current site (don't wait for the rebuild)
Phase 1: core rebuild (what's in it)
Phase 2+: enhancements
For each phase: outcomes the owner will see, rough complexity, dependencies.

## 8. What we need from you
The [To confirm with owner] list turned into a meeting agenda: access,
decisions, content ownership questions, business goals, analytics access.

## Appendix A — Full findings register
The complete table: ID · Area · Finding · Evidence · Severity · Effort · Recommendation.

## Appendix B — Page inventory summary
(or reference the separate inventory file)
```

Writing guidance:
- Lead every issue with the consequence, not the technology. "Visitors on phones can't read your menu" then "(the site lacks responsive design)". 
- Estimated complexity is S/M/L effort, never invented prices or hours — pricing is a separate commercial conversation unless the user asks you to draft one.
- Keep the Confirmed/Inferred/To-confirm labels, but render them in plain language in the body ("we observed…", "this suggests…", "you'll need to confirm…"). Full labels stay in the appendix register.
- If screenshots were captured, place them beside the finding they evidence with a caption. If not, cite exact URLs so the owner can look themselves.
